{% include lore-block.html chapter="06" label="polite forever" %}

The graphs were beautiful. That was the first thing wrong with them.

```
[ALERT] INC-0006 — Reconciliation backlog static for 90m
[INFO ] recovery workers: 3/3 running
[INFO ] cpu: 94% sustained
[INFO ] state transitions: 2,114,880 in window
[WARN ] committed transactions in window: 41,003
[WARN ] commits are not evenly distributed across workers
```

Two million state transitions. Three commits.

Andy scrolled the transition log until the lines stopped meaning anything: claim,
yield, reclaim, yield, claim. Two workers passing the same batch back and forth
with the manners of people stuck in a doorway.

"Not a deadlock," he said, before Bob could.

Bob sat down anyway. "No. A deadlock stops. You can see a deadlock from orbit —
nothing moves, everyone panics, someone kills a process, we all go to lunch."

He gestured at the CPU graph, flat and high.

"This one looks like the team working late. It'll look like that until the
quarter ends."

Andy pulled the commit counters. One worker had done everything. One had done
almost nothing. One had done nothing at all, and had been doing nothing at all
with tremendous energy.

<section id="lore-reveal" class="mt-5 hidden">
{% include lore-block.html chapter="06" label="after the livelock" %}

`w-merge` and `w-drain` were each other's trigger.

`w-merge` claimed a batch and began merging. `w-drain` saw a claimed batch aging
and reclaimed it for the drain path. `w-merge` saw its claim revoked, backed off,
and claimed again. Each one was correctly implementing a recovery policy. Each
one was the reason the other had something to recover from.

`w-drain` got three transactions through in ninety minutes, in the gaps.
`w-merge` got none. `w-reap`, on a five second backoff and talking to nobody, did
all forty-one thousand.

The fix was four lines: one of them had to stop being polite.

Andy filed it, then went back to the transition log, because two million lines of
claim-yield had produced a handful that were not claim-yield. Torn writes, Bob
had called them. Andy reassembled them anyway, in order, by offset.

{% include lore-block.html chapter="06" label="the index" %}

He had seen that shape before. Five days on this floor and twice now a line had
arrived in a log addressed to nobody, in a format no service emitted.

He checked the audit index, which is authoritative, which is the entire point of
an audit index.

```
offset 0x41f0–0x4230   no record
```

The lines were in the log. The index said the log had never been written to.

Andy copied both to his laptop.
